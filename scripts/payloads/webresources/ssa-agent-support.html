SSA agent support

Frontline guide for Social Security service calls and escalations

Use this page to triage caller intent, collect the right facts, route sensitive issues, and point the public to official SSA and OIG resources. The fraud area is based on SSA's public fraud prevention and reporting guidance.

SSA says fraud includes intentional false statements, concealed material facts, SSN misuse, identity theft, employee impersonation, representative payee misuse, and misleading SSA-related communications.

Call triage

Start with the caller's real need

  1. Confirm the category.

    Benefit question, account access, missing payment, identity concern, suspected fraud, representative payee issue, overpayment, appeal, or local office request.

  2. Protect personal information.

    Use approved identity proofing before discussing records. Do not ask callers to say an SSN aloud when a safer channel or partial verification is available.

  3. Capture the minimum useful facts.

    Who is affected, what happened, when it started, how the caller learned about it, what documents or notices exist, and whether funds or personal information are at risk.

  4. Route to the owner.

    Use SSA service channels for benefit and account service. Use OIG reporting paths when the allegation involves fraud, waste, abuse, or employee impersonation.

Quick signals

Escalate when you hear

  • Threats or urgencyCaller was told benefits will stop unless they pay, transfer money, or reveal credentials.
  • Identity exposureSSN was used for accounts, credit, work, claims, or tax activity without permission.
  • Benefit misuseA representative payee may be using funds for something other than the beneficiary's needs.
  • False affiliationA website, social account, mailer, or caller appears to pose as SSA or charge for free SSA services.

Fraud support area

Suspected Social Security fraud

SSA describes Social Security fraud as intentionally giving false information, misrepresenting information, or concealing important facts that could affect benefit entitlement or eligibility. Agents should keep the caller focused on observable facts and route reports to OIG.

Examples that belong in the fraud path

  • False claims or concealed eventsA person may have made false statements, hidden work, hidden a death, or concealed eligibility facts.
  • SSN misuseSomeone may be working, filing claims, opening accounts, or receiving benefits under another person's SSN.
  • Impersonation or briberySomeone claims to be an SSA employee, demands payment, or offers improper help.

What to collect before referral

  • People and identifiersNames, relationship to beneficiary, partial identifiers allowed by policy, caller contact information, and affected program.
  • Timeline and proofDates, notices, payment records, message screenshots, phone numbers, web addresses, envelopes, and account activity.
  • Immediate riskMoney lost, benefits redirected, direct deposit changes, threatened safety, or ongoing contact by a suspicious party.
Fraud reporting route

Use OIG's online fraud report for allegations involving Social Security fraud, waste, or abuse. The OIG fraud hotline is 1-800-269-0271, available 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. ET, Monday through Friday, excluding federal holidays.

Identity theft

When the caller says their SSN was exposed or used

Separate exposure from confirmed misuse. SSA's fraud page points people to the FTC when an SSN has been used to open accounts, obtain credit, make purchases, or commit other identity theft.

  • Confirmed misuseRefer the person to IdentityTheft.gov for an FTC Identity Theft Report and recovery plan.
  • Lost, stolen, shared, or breached SSNPoint to IdentityTheft.gov/Info-Lost-or-Stolen for protection steps.
  • Account protectionDiscuss appropriate SSA account protection steps through approved SSA channels, including local office support when needed.

Representative payee

When benefits may be misused

If someone receives benefits on behalf of a beneficiary, those funds must be used for the beneficiary's needs. SSA says it investigates misuse allegations and may help replace the payee, pay the beneficiary directly, and seek recovery of misused funds.

Agent prompt

"I can document what you are seeing and help route this for review. Please describe how the benefits are being used, who controls the funds, and any records, receipts, or notices you have."

Misleading SSA communications

Section 1140 style concerns

SSA warns that it is unlawful for people or companies to mislead the public by pretending to be associated with or endorsed by SSA, including charging for services SSA provides free of charge.

  • Suspicious website or social accountCapture screenshots, web address, social link, and how the caller found it.
  • Email or text messageSave the entire message and links. Do not instruct the caller to click suspicious links.
  • Postal mail or phone solicitationKeep the envelope and inserts, or record caller ID, callback number, company name, and script details.

Documentation checklist

Close the interaction cleanly

  1. Summarize the concern.

    Use neutral language. Attribute claims to the caller unless verified by SSA records.

  2. Record official referrals.

    Include the exact public resource shared, such as SSA.gov/fraud, OIG fraud report, IdentityTheft.gov, or local office locator.

  3. State next expectations.

    OIG reviews allegations but generally cannot share law enforcement record details with the reporting person.

  4. Flag urgent risk.

    Escalate according to internal policy when benefits, safety, direct deposit, or identity controls may be at immediate risk.
